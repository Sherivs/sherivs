## Part A — Memorandum of Understanding

**Between**

1. **Borno State Government**, acting through the **Ministry of Education, Science, Technology and
   Innovation (MOESTI)**, together with its agencies named in Schedule 1 (including the State
   Universal Basic Education Board (BOSUBEB), the Teaching Service Board and the State
   Examinations Board) (the "**State**"); and
2. **[Sherivs legal entity name, RC number]**, a company registered in Nigeria, with its
   registered office at [address] ("**Sherivs**").

### 1. Purpose
1.1 To pilot and, if successful, scale the Sherivs school operating platform in Borno State
public schools and to provide the State with a government console
(`borno.gov.sherivs.com`) for planning, inspection, circulars, teacher deployment and
statistical reporting.
1.2 This MoU records intent and the framework for a later definitive services agreement. Save
for clauses 8 (Confidentiality), 9 (Data protection), 12 (Governing law) and 13 (Dispute
resolution), it is not legally binding. [Counsel to confirm.]

### 2. Scope of the pilot
2.1 Pilot schools: [number] schools in [LGAs, e.g. Maiduguri, Jere, Biu], listed in Schedule 2,
selected by the State.
2.2 Pilot period: one academic term, from [date] to [date].
2.3 Modules in scope: enrolment and attendance, results, school profile and public website,
communications to parents, and the government console (aggregates, inspections, circulars,
teacher deployment, exports).
2.4 Out of scope unless agreed in writing: payment collection, biometric data, CCTV, location
tracking of learners.

### 3. Responsibilities of the State
3.1 Nominate a project lead and one focal officer per pilot LGA.
3.2 Provide the official school register and school codes, and staff lists from BOSUBEB / the
Teaching Service Board, through a secure channel agreed under Part B.
3.3 Communicate the pilot to head teachers, school-based management committees and parents,
including the privacy notice in Schedule 4.
3.4 Nominate and authorise government console users in writing (clause 6).

### 4. Responsibilities of Sherivs
4.1 Provide the platform, hosting, support during school hours, and training (train-the-trainer
for LGA desk officers and head teachers).
4.2 Provide offline-capable data capture where connectivity is poor. [Confirm availability date.]
4.3 Keep the platform secure and comply with Part B.
4.4 Report weekly to the State project lead on adoption and incidents.

### 5. Fees
5.1 The pilot is provided at [no cost / cost of ₦___ covering devices and training].
5.2 After the pilot, the parties intend to negotiate an annual State licence for public schools.
Private schools contract with Sherivs directly. [Pricing schedule to be agreed; subject to State
procurement rules.]

### 6. Government console access
6.1 Access is invite-only. Only officers named by the State in writing receive an invitation.
6.2 Each officer is scoped to an agency and a jurisdiction (State, LGA). Officers see
aggregated statistics; small groups (fewer than 10 learners) are suppressed.
6.3 Access to identifiable learner data by government officers requires a documented lawful
basis and purpose under Part B and is logged.
6.4 Officers must use multi-factor authentication. The State must notify Sherivs within 2 working
days when an officer leaves post so access can be revoked.

### 7. Intellectual property
7.1 Sherivs retains all rights in the platform. The State and schools retain all rights in their
data. Sherivs may use anonymised, aggregated statistics to improve the service, never to identify
a learner, teacher or school. [Counsel to review.]

### 8. Confidentiality
Each party keeps the other's confidential information confidential for the term and [3] years
after, subject to law and freedom-of-information obligations.

### 9. Data protection
The parties shall comply with the NDPA and enter into the data-sharing and processing agreement in
Part B, which prevails over this MoU on data matters.

### 10. Term and termination
10.1 This MoU runs from signature until the end of the pilot plus [3] months, unless replaced by a
definitive agreement.
10.2 Either party may terminate on [30] days' written notice. On termination clause B.11 (return
and deletion) applies.

### 11. Publicity
No press release or use of the other party's name or logo without prior written consent.

### 12. Governing law

### 13. Dispute resolution

## Part B — NDPA Data-Sharing and Processing Agreement

### B.1 Roles of the parties [Counsel to confirm]
(a) For public schools, the **State** (through MOESTI / BOSUBEB as the responsible authority for
the schools) is the **data controller** of learner, parent and staff records held on the platform.
(b) **Sherivs** is a **data processor** acting on the State's documented instructions, except for
platform account and security data, for which Sherivs is an independent controller.
(c) For the government console, the State is controller of the aggregated statistics it uses.
(d) Private schools that opt in to share aggregates are separate controllers; sharing with the State
is governed by their own notice and opt-in recorded on the platform.

### B.2 Lawful basis
(a) Processing of learner records for education: performance of a task carried out in the public
interest / exercise of official authority vested in the State [NDPA s.25 — counsel to cite exact
paragraph], and NDPA s.31(4)(b) (education carried out by or under a professional duty of
confidentiality) so parental consent is not the basis for core education records.
(b) Optional processing (photos on public websites, marketing, non-essential communications):
**consent of the parent or legal guardian** (NDPA s.31(1)), with age and consent verification
(s.31(2)–(3)). Consent is recorded per purpose on the platform and can be withdrawn.
(c) Sensitive data (health, religion, disability) only where strictly necessary and under NDPA
s.30, with the safeguards in B.6.

### B.3 Categories of data subjects and data
Schedule 3 lists: learners, parents/guardians, teachers and staff, government officers. For each:
fields collected, purpose, retention, and who may access.

### B.4 Purpose limitation
Data is processed only for: school administration, teaching and learning, statutory reporting
(including the Annual School Census), planning and inspection by the State. No sale of data, no
advertising to learners, no profiling of learners for commercial purposes.

### B.5 Data minimisation for government use
(a) The government console shows aggregates by default, with small-cell suppression (<10).
(b) Identifiable learner data is shared with the State only for a named purpose approved in
writing by the State's DPO and logged (e.g. a specific inspection or safeguarding case).
(c) Learner location, home address and guardian contact details are never exported to the
government console. [Given security conditions in parts of the State — counsel and the State to
confirm.]

### B.6 Security measures (Schedule 5)
Encryption in transit (TLS) and at rest; row-level security isolating each school; MFA for
administrators, heads and government officers; append-only audit logs; break-glass access with
reason, alert and expiry; least-privilege, time-limited roles; backups; annual penetration test;
incident response plan.

### B.7 Sub-processors
Current: cloud database and authentication (Supabase), web hosting (Vercel), DNS/CDN (Cloudflare),
SMS/voice (Twilio), [payments (Paystack) — out of pilot scope]. Sherivs gives [30] days' notice of
changes; the State may object.

### B.8 International transfers
Some sub-processors host data outside Nigeria. Transfers rely on [NDPA Part VIII basis — adequacy,
appropriate safeguards or contractual clauses — counsel to confirm]. Sherivs will document each
transfer in Schedule 6 and offer [Nigeria / Africa region hosting] where available.

### B.9 Data subject rights
Requests are routed to the controller (State or school). Sherivs assists within [5] working days.
The platform supports access, rectification and erasure workflows.

### B.10 Personal data breach
Sherivs notifies the State without undue delay and within [24] hours of becoming aware. The
controller notifies the NDPC within 72 hours where required (NDPA s.40) and data subjects where
there is high risk.

### B.11 Retention, return and deletion
Retention per Schedule 3 and the State's records policy. On termination, Sherivs returns data in an
open format (CSV/JSON) within [30] days and then deletes it, except where law requires retention,
and certifies deletion.

### B.12 Audit
The State or an NDPC-licensed DPCO may audit compliance on [30] days' notice, once a year, and after
a breach.

### B.13 DPIA
A Data Protection Impact Assessment is completed before go-live (NDPA s.28) and reviewed at the end
of the pilot. Sherivs supplies the technical sections.

### B.14 Data Protection Officers
State DPO designation and contact routing: to be supplied by the State outside this repository. Sherivs DPO: see the [current designation](../ndpa-overview.md#current-dpo-designation); contact routing is supplied at runtime.
