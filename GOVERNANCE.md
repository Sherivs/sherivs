1. PURPOSE
CoreGrid is intended to become a universal Reality Intelligence Operating System capable of representing, understanding, reasoning about, simulating, governing, and acting upon complex reality.
Such a system cannot safely evolve through informal decisions alone.
As CoreGrid grows, it will encounter:
• new blueprints,
• new domains,
• new reality models,
• new engines,
• new phases,
• cross-phase architectures,
• new models,
• new agents,
• new infrastructure,
• new contracts,
• new data,
• new integrations,
• new risks,
• new failures,
• new evidence,
• new requirements,
• new technologies,
• and previously unknown architectural problems.
Governance exists to make this evolution:
• coherent,
• traceable,
• authorized,
• evidence-based,
• secure,
• reversible where practical,
• understandable,
• and continuously verifiable.
Governance must therefore answer:
Who may decide what, under which authority, using what evidence, within which boundaries, with what consequences, and how do we know the resulting system remains aligned with CoreGrid?

DOMAIN EXPANSION
ARCHITECTURAL EVOLUTION
MODEL REPLACEMENT

Governance must therefore avoid both extremes:
Under-governance
Where:
• agents invent architecture,
• developers bypass boundaries,
• blueprints become implementation automatically,
• undocumented assumptions become law,
• secrets leak,
• progress is fabricated,
• and architecture drifts silently.
Over-governance
Where:
• every trivial change requires a committee,
• experimentation becomes impossible,
• documents multiply unnecessarily,
• decisions become bureaucratic,
• engineering velocity collapses,
• and governance becomes detached from reality.
CoreGrid requires proportionate governance.

5. AUTHORITY IS NOT THE SAME AS TRUTH
Governance authority determines who or what may make a decision.
Evidence determines what happened.
These are different.
For example:
text
Architecture says X.
Implementation behaves as Y.
Evidence demonstrates Y.

The evidence does not automatically rewrite architecture.
Instead:
text
Evidence
↓
Architecture discrepancy
↓
Analysis
↓
Decision
↓
Architecture update
↓
Implementation reconciliation

This distinction prevents implementation accidents from becoming architecture by default.

6. CORE GOVERNANCE PRINCIPLES
CoreGrid governance follows these principles.
6.1 Reality Over Appearance
The system must represent observed reality rather than desired narratives.
6.2 Evidence Over Assumption
Important claims require appropriate evidence.
6.3 Architecture Over Convenience
Engineering convenience must not silently redefine architecture.
6.4 Explicit Authority Over Implicit Authority
A worker does not gain authority merely because it has technical access.
6.5 Human Agency Over Unbounded Autonomy
Intelligence does not automatically imply authority.
6.6 Controlled Evolution Over Architectural Stagnation
CoreGrid must evolve when evidence demonstrates that evolution is necessary.
6.7 Coherence Over Fragmentation
Capabilities should be consolidated when they share a genuine authority domain.
6.8 Capability Density Over Component Count
More components do not automatically mean more intelligence.
6.9 Explicit Contracts Over Hidden Coupling
Dependencies must be visible.
6.10 Historical Integrity Over Narrative Cleanliness
Past states must not be rewritten merely to make progress look cleaner.
6.11 Provider Neutrality
No external AI provider becomes CoreGrid architecture.
6.12 Evidence Before Completion
Completion must correspond to demonstrated system state.
6.13 Uncertainty Over Fabricated Certainty
Unknown must remain representable.
6.14 Proportionate Governance
Governance intensity must correspond to impact and risk.
6.15 Reversibility Where Practical
High-impact decisions should identify reversal or recovery mechanisms where possible.

• governance capability,
• infrastructure capability,

• reserved future capability,

• What is outside?
• Who owns the boundary?
• What crosses it?
