## Context

ADR-005 ruled the erasure *mechanism* (Option 2 — redaction-with-tombstone; erasure is itself an append-only audit event referencing the tombstoned record IDs, the retention policy ID, the authorizing identity, and **the erasure scope**) and deliberately left two questions open:

1. **Cross-tenant erasure semantics** (ADR-005 UNKNOWN 3): whether a subject spanning multiple tenants — e.g. a staff member employed at two schools, or a parent with children enrolled in two institutions — is erased per-tenant or globally. ADR-005 flagged this as dependent on the tenant_id type ruling (COND-003) and the identity model.
2. **Backup-expiry discipline** (ADR-005 UNKNOWN 4, implementation path step 4): "backups expire on the retention schedule; restore must re-apply redaction state" was stated as a discipline coordinated with J-042, with the verifiable invariant and its owner undefined.

The question is concrete, not hypothetical. CoreGrid is multi-tenant and institutional (AGENTS §13): the same human legitimately exists as a subject in several tenants, and RETENTION_POLICY_SCHEDULE.json already keys every policy by **tenant class** (`education-institution | organization | platform`) with per-tenant-class policy IDs (`RP-AUDIT-EDU`, `RP-AUDIT-ORG`, `RP-AUDIT-PLT`, …). The schedule's `meta.provenance.crossTenantErasure` note explicitly defers the scope ruling to an ADR and records that "every policy here is scoped per tenant class and erasure scope is recorded in the erasure event" — this ADR instantiates that `erasureScope` field.

Two structural facts constrain the answer:

- **Tenant isolation is absolute (AGENTS §13, §26, §17).** Every request carries tenant context; RLS is deny-by-default on tenant-sensitive data; server functions resolve tenant server-side and never trust client tenant IDs (§26). A mechanism that sweeps one subject's records across tenant boundaries in a single data operation would require a cross-tenant read scope — a silent permission expansion the Constitution forbids creating (§8, §37).
- **There is no portable cross-tenant subject key (COND-003, ADR-001).** The canonical tenant identifier is an opaque `text` ref (`CHECK length 1..128`) — an addressing scheme, not a joinable global identity. The legacy Scholar runtime keys on `school_id uuid` against a different table (`schools.id`), with the uuid→text seam established at the adapter boundary. "The same subject" across tenants is only resolvable through tenant-scoped identity records (`cg_identity.tenant_id`) and membership rows via an **authorized, provenance-carrying resolution step** — never a schema-level join.

The two axes must not be conflated. The retention schedule's per-policy `erasure.scope` field describes **what values are erased** (scope-of-values, per record family). This ADR defines the complementary vocabulary for **where the erasure executes** (scope-of-execution, per tenant boundary). Both appear on erasure events; they are independent fields.

## Options

### Option 1 — Per-tenant erasure only (tenant-scoped policy ID, no subject-level construct)

Erasure exists exclusively as a per-tenant operation under that tenant's policy ID. A multi-tenant subject must file a separate erasure request in each tenant that holds them; no subject-level request record exists anywhere.

- **Consequences:** maximally isolated and the simplest to implement — it is exactly today's per-tenant-class policy schedule with nothing added. Costs: the lawful erasure duty (Constitution §19: the *subject* requests erasure, not "erase me in tenant X") is shifted onto the subject as an operational burden; there is no verifiable record that every tenant holding the subject was even notified — completeness is unknowable by construction; the fact "this person requested erasure" is itself personal data, and under this option it lives only in one tenant's audit, invisible to the others and to platform governance.

### Option 2 — Global erasure with per-tenant tombstone events (single global operation)

One global erasure operation, addressed to the subject, sweeps all tenants and appends tombstone events everywhere in a single coordinated transaction.

- **Consequences:** the subject files one request and completeness is, in principle, global. Costs: the mechanism requires a platform actor with cross-tenant **data read scope** to find the subject's records in every tenant — a permission expansion that violates the tenant-isolation invariants (§13, §17) and cannot be built without a cross-tenant subject key, which COND-003's opaque-text ruling and the legacy `school_id uuid` seam both deny exists mechanically. It also conflates two different things: the *request* (a governance act by the subject) and the *execution* (a data operation that must be authorized, audited, and RLS-contained **within one tenant** per §27). A single global transaction across tenants would also make one tenant's erasure event dependent on another tenant's execution — coupling tenant audit trails to each other and breaking the per-tenant provenance AGENTS §12 requires.

### Option 3 — Hybrid: global-by-request, per-tenant-by-execution (via the COND-003 tenant_id ruling)

The **erasure request is subject-level** and is recorded once, at the governance layer (platform side, AGENTS §14 — the Control Plane is not a tenant and is the only actor legitimately positioned to hold a subject-level record). The request carries a **subject-resolution step**: an authorized, provenance-recorded operation (§12) that maps the subject to the tenant-scoped identity handles (`cg_identity.tenant_id` text refs; Scholar `school_id uuid` through the adapter seam) of the tenants that hold them, producing a **tenant manifest**. Execution then decomposes into **independent per-tenant erasure operations**, each: authorized within its own tenant (§26/§27), scoped to exactly one tenant boundary, executed under that tenant-class's policy ID from RETENTION_POLICY_SCHEDULE.json (`RP-*-EDU` / `RP-*-ORG`, or `RP-*-PLT` for platform-operated records), and recorded as that tenant's own append-only tombstone event with `erasureScope` set per the vocabulary below. The request record carries the fan-out manifest — which tenants were resolved into scope and the reference to each per-tenant erasure event.

- **Consequences:** tenant isolation is preserved mechanically — no data operation ever crosses a tenant boundary; each per-tenant tombstone event is fully provenance-carrying (§12) and its tenant attribution survives erasure in full (ADR-005 "what may NOT be forgotten"). Completeness becomes **verifiable per request** rather than assumed: the request is CLOSED only when every tenant in its resolved manifest has a well-formed per-tenant erasure event (the fan-out completeness invariant below). Honest costs: "global" erasure is only as complete as subject resolution — a subject existing under unlinkable identity values in different tenants (deliberately, per tenant isolation) will not be auto-linked, so the resolved manifest can miss tenants the subject knows about; the subject can remedy this by asserting them, but the guarantee is "all tenants *resolved* were erased", never "all tenants *in existence* were erased" — this bound must be disclosed in the V1CAP-066 contract. Second cost: per-tenant retention clocks stay independent — the same subject's records expire on different dates in different tenants (same subject, different policy IDs); a global request erases *now* in every scoped tenant regardless of natural expiry, but natural expiry remains per-tenant, per-family.

## Recommendation

**Option 3 — the hybrid ("global-by-request, per-tenant-by-execution") — as the V1 ruling.** Option 1 abandons the subject and makes lawful erasure completeness unknowable; Option 2 requires a cross-tenant data scope and a global subject key that COND-003's ruling structurally denies. Option 3 matches the substrate as it actually exists: subject-level intent at the governance layer, tenant-contained execution everywhere else — the same decomposition the Constitution applies to every other cross-cutting concern (authorization §16, server functions §27).

### Erasure-scope vocabulary (binding; recorded here as a table, deliberately NOT as code)

Machine-checkable vocabulary for the `erasureScope` field of erasure events and request records. Per MISSION H2-09 file ownership this vocabulary is recorded **in this ADR as a table only** — no validator code is added to `scripts/verification`; wiring it into the verify pipeline is the V1CAP-066 contract's verification duty (see Remaining Work). It is consistent with RETENTION_POLICY_SCHEDULE.json's vocabularies and is the instantiation of the `erasureScope` field its `reconciliationModel.erasureEvent` already names.

| Vocabulary | Values | Meaning / binding rule |
|---|---|---|
| `erasureScope` (on **executed** erasure events) | `tenant-scoped` | Erasure executes within exactly one tenant boundary under that tenant-class policy ID (`RP-*-EDU` / `RP-*-ORG`). The only valid scope for tenant-held records. |
| | `platform-scoped` | Erasure of platform-operated personal records under a platform-class policy ID (`RP-*-PLT`). The Control Plane is not a tenant (AGENTS §14) but its personal records still require an authorized, audited tombstone. |
| | `global` | **NOT valid on an executed erasure event.** Valid only on the subject-level erasure *request* record, where it means "request intends all tenants holding the subject". Execution is always decomposed into `tenant-scoped` / `platform-scoped` events. |
| `erasureScope` (on **request** records) | `tenant-scoped` \| `platform-scoped` \| `global` | The subject-level request declares intent. A `global` request MUST carry a resolved tenant manifest before any execution. |

**Machine-checkable rules (verified by the pipeline, not by code in this repository today):**

| # | Invariant | Checked against |
|---|---|---|
| E-1 | Every executed erasure event carries exactly one `erasureScope` ∈ {`tenant-scoped`, `platform-scoped`} and exactly one retention policy ID resolvable in RETENTION_POLICY_SCHEDULE.json (same recordFamily and tenantClass). | Verify pipeline (V1CAP-066 verificationOwner per ADR-005) |
| E-2 | No executed erasure event carries `erasureScope: global`; a `global` value appears only on request records. | Verify pipeline |
| E-3 | A `global` subject-level request is CLOSED only when every tenant in its resolved manifest has a corresponding well-formed `tenant-scoped` erasure event (the fan-out completeness invariant). | Verify pipeline |
| E-4 | Tombstone-not-delete holds at every scope: no scope value authorizes mutation or deletion of any record; erasure is always append-an-event + redact-through-the-projection-layer (ADR-005 Option 2, unchanged). | Verify pipeline + existing family `tombstoneInvariant` declarations in the schedule inventory |

### Backup-expiry discipline (coordinated with J-042)

ADR-005 implementation path step 4 stated the discipline; this ADR defines the invariant and its owner. The substrate facts: backups are **redundant copies, not the canonical immutable substrate** — AGENTS §28's tombstone-not-delete binds the canonical append-only records, and ADR-005's "what may NOT be forgotten" list is carried by the substrate, not by any copy of it. Destroying an expired backup artifact is therefore not record destruction; conversely, a backup that outlives the lawful retention of the personal data it contains *is* an erasure violation (the values remain readable in the copy). Erasure reaches backups through **time**, not through mutation:

| # | Invariant | Statement |
|---|---|---|
| B-1 | **Backup manifest completeness.** Every backup artifact containing any personal-data-bearing record family MUST carry a manifest recording: `takenAt`, per-record-family retention policy IDs (resolvable in RETENTION_POLICY_SCHEDULE.json), and per-family tombstone/redaction projection state at backup time. | RESTORE_RECOVERY_RUNBOOK_MR101 §1 already obligates exactly this for production backups; this ADR makes it the J-042 contract requirement |
| B-2 | **Backup-lifetime bound.** The manifest MUST record `expiresAt` ≤ the earliest possible retention deadline among the personal data the backup contains (per-family policy `retentionDays` / `maxRetentionDays` from the schedule, computed from `takenAt` and record ages). A backup MUST never outlive the lawful retention of the most short-lived personal data it holds (e.g. a backup containing `ai-snapshot` content expires within that family's 180-day window). Expired backup artifacts are destroyed as redundant copies. | Phase 21 (J-042 owner) computes from phase 14's schedule |
| B-3 | **Backups read through the redaction layer.** A backup taken *after* a record's erasure contains the tombstoned rendering, never pre-erasure personal values — backup reads go through the redaction projection, never raw. | V1CAP-066 contract; verify pipeline |
| B-4 | **Restore re-applies redaction state (the J-042 verifiable invariant).** Any restore MUST replay the tombstone/redaction projection state recorded in the backup manifest (B-1). Post-restore, the ADR-005 erasure invariant MUST hold over the restored dataset: *for every tombstoned record, the record exists, is non-readable as personal data through the redaction layer, and its erasure event is present and well-formed.* A restore that resurrects pre-erasure personal values is a FINDING (exit 1) — the same machine-checkable shape the restore drill already produces (`scripts/restore-recovery-drill.mjs`; exit 0 = PASS, 1 = FINDING, 2 = blocked on credentials). | Restore drill discipline per RESTORE_RECOVERY_RUNBOOK_MR101; verify pipeline |

Honest residual exposure, stated rather than hidden: a subject erased **after** a backup was taken remains readable inside that existing artifact until its `expiresAt` (B-2). Erasure reaches such backups on the backup's expiry schedule, not instantaneously; the maximum residual window is bounded by B-2 and MUST be disclosed in the V1CAP-066 contract. This is the same policy-level (not bit-level) limitation ADR-005 recorded for the redaction layer, bounded here by time instead of by access control.

**Ownership (Golden Questions answer in advance):** phase 21 (infrastructure) owns backup tooling, manifest enforcement, and expiry destruction as the J-042 capability owner (with phase 14 supplying the schedule numbers that compute `expiresAt`); phase 09 (audit immutability boundary) owns the rule that backup destruction never touches canonical records; the verify pipeline (V1CAP-066's verificationOwner, per ADR-005) owns checks E-1–E-4 and B-4; RESTORE_RECOVERY_RUNBOOK_MR101 remains the operational instrument and its manifest obligations are now contract-bound by B-1.

**Consistency statement:** nothing in this ADR deletes or mutates a canonical record at any scope. Tombstone-not-delete holds for subject records in every tenant, for platform records, for the erasure events themselves, and across backup/restore — where it is joined by its temporal twin: *what the substrate never forgets, redundant copies must not outlive.*

## Golden Questions

- **WHO?** The subject requests (a governance act, recorded once at the platform governance layer); each tenant's authorized erasure operator executes its own tombstone within its own boundary (per-tenant authorization, §26/§27); subject resolution is an authorized, provenance-carrying server-side operation (§12), never a client-asserted tenant list (§26). Phase 03 (tenancy/data classification) owns this ADR's vocabulary with phase 14 (retention policy) and phase 09 (audit immutability boundary); phase 21 owns backup tooling, manifests, and expiry (J-042, L-H-027); the verify pipeline owns E-1–E-4 and B-4.
- **WHAT?** Executed erasure is always `tenant-scoped` or `platform-scoped` under a resolvable per-tenant-class policy ID; `global` exists only as request intent and is decomposed; a global request is CLOSED only on a complete per-tenant fan-out. Backups carry manifests (policy IDs + redaction state + `expiresAt`), expire on the retention schedule, are read through the redaction layer, and restores re-apply redaction state so no restore resurrects erased personal data.
- **WHERE?** Subject-level request records and fan-out manifests at the platform governance layer; per-tenant erasure events in each tenant's append-only audit substrate; backup manifests and `expiresAt` in the phase-21 backup tooling; invariant checks E-1–E-4/B-4 in the verify pipeline and the restore drill (`scripts/restore-recovery-drill.mjs` discipline).
- **WHY?** ADR-005 left both questions open (its UNKNOWNs 3 and 4) and RETENTION_POLICY_SCHEDULE.json explicitly deferred the scope ruling here. A platform where one human legitimately spans several institutional tenants cannot operate lawfully without an erasure path that neither crosses tenant boundaries (§13) nor resurrects erased data from backups (J-042) — and COND-003's opaque-text tenant ref means any "global join" mechanism would be invented infrastructure with a permission-expansion cost (§8).
- **UNDER WHICH POLICY?** ADR-005 (parent mechanism ruling — unchanged); ADR-001/COND-003 (text tenant ref: subject resolution is an authorized operation, not a schema join); Constitution §19 (subject erasure duty per J-039) and §9 (per-tenant aggregates remain Intelligence, never Reality); AGENTS §12 (provenance of request, resolution, and fan-out manifest), §13 (tenant context on every operation), §16 (decision reconstruction survives erasure at every scope), §17 (RLS deny-by-default contains execution in each tenant), §19 (expired purpose redacted, including in memory/AI surfaces per tenant policy), §26 (never trust client tenant IDs — the manifest is server-resolved), §27 (each per-tenant erasure is a server function: authenticate → resolve tenant → authorize → validate → execute → audit), §28 (tombstone-not-delete at every scope; backup destruction is copy expiry, not record destruction), §31 (security tests must prove tenant A's erasure never touches tenant B, an out-of-manifest tenant is never erased by another tenant's request, and restore never resurrects erased values); J-042; L-H-026/L-H-027; RETENTION_POLICY_SCHEDULE.json (`reconciliationModel`, per-tenant-class policy IDs, `provenance.crossTenantErasure` deferral); RESTORE_RECOVERY_RUNBOOK_MR101 (executed evidence, VERDICT=PASS).

## UNKNOWNs

- **COND-003 operator ruling is still pending** (ADR-001 is a PROPOSED dossier): this ADR's decomposition principle survives either tenant_id outcome (it relies on tenant-scoped identity, not on the identifier type), but the *mechanics* of subject resolution (helper signatures, seam handling) finalize only after the operator rules; if uuid-as-canonical were adopted against the dossier's recommendation, the resolution step re-opens in detail, not in principle.
- **Cross-seam subject resolution is undecided:** how a subject is linked between the legacy Scholar identity world (`school_id uuid`, `public.users`) and the universal one (`cg_identity.tenant_id` text) for fan-out purposes depends on the I-001 identity split landing; until then a global request's manifest can only cover tenants resolved within one identifier world per resolution pass, and repeated passes may be required.
- **The subject-level erasure request record's own lifecycle is not yet scheduled:** it is personal data ("this person asked to be erased"), likely platform-class (`RP-*-PLT` families), but the platform-class `identity-profile` policy detail and the record family for governance request records are not declared in RETENTION_POLICY_SCHEDULE.json — flagged for the V1CAP-066 contract.
- **Mechanical backup-expiry enforcement has no owner-authored tooling yet:** the J-042 capability (V1CAP-074) is impl=false in the hardening JSON; the restore drill proves the *verification discipline* (logical restore, checksums, evidence-integrity fingerprint), not storage-level TTL machinery. B-2's `expiresAt` computation and destruction automation are phase-21 deliverables.
- **Whether restore-time re-redaction (B-4) is testable in V1 CI without live credentials** (J-041 issue) remains open, carried forward from ADR-005's backup UNKNOWN: the drill requires serialized canonical access, so CI wiring alongside the RLS isolation battery is a phase-21 task, not yet proven.
- **Whether read replicas and change streams (realtime security, AGENTS §35) inherit the backup-expiry discipline or need their own erasure-propagation rule** is assumed yes-by-policy and mechanically unverified in the corpus — carried forward unchanged from ADR-005's UNKNOWN 5; this ADR neither resolves nor narrows it.
